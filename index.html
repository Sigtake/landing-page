One row per incident, not forty

The whole loop between “it fired” and “it’s fixed”: ingestion, deduplication, routing and acknowledgement.

  • One fingerprint per incidentSource, title and severity decide what counts as a repeat, scoped to one project. Staging and production stay two incidents.
  • Notified at 1, 10, 25, 50 and 100Then every hundredth. Forty-seven repeats are three notifications, not forty-seven.
  • Acknowledge, discuss and resolve in the incidentComments with @mentions, and updates that stream in live so everyone sees the same thing.
  • The API key picks the projectProduction and staging keys land alerts in their own project. There is no project id to pass.
  • The team_code picks the teamEvery alert belongs to one team. Teams can span projects.
  • The team’s channels in that project deliver itConfigure a channel once and reuse it across teams with per-team toggles.
  • Email, Slack, Microsoft Teams and in-appIn-app is always on; the rest you switch per team.
  • One Slack workspace, a channel per projectRepeats reply in the thread of the first card, and the card changes colour as the alert is acknowledged and resolved.
  • A broken channel never blocks the othersEmail retries up to three times. Discord and Google Chat are in progress.

Before you ask

Do I have to replace my current monitoring?

No. Sigtake sits downstream of what you already run. Anything that can send an HTTP request with its own JSON body can post an alert: a cron job, a CI step, the SDKs. Tools with a fixed webhook format, like Alertmanager, need a small relay in between.

How does it decide two alerts are the same incident?

A fingerprint over source, title and severity, scoped to one project. The same failure in staging and production stays two separate incidents.

Who gets notified?

The team named by the alert’s team code, through that team’s channels in the alert’s project. In-app always lands, so nothing is lost while a channel is misconfigured.

What happens if a channel is down?

Email retries up to three times with exponential backoff, and a delivery that still fails is marked as failed. A broken Slack or Microsoft Teams channel never blocks the email, and in-app always lands.
